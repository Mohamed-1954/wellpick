# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Small service business owners and operators — studios, agencies, freelancers with
clients — evaluating no-code tools for client intake, client portals, and team
operations. They are busy, semi-technical at most, and comparison-shopping before
spending. The job: pick the right tool, or confidently decide none is needed.

## Product Purpose

Wellpick (renamed from The Client Stack on 3 Oct 2026) is an independent affiliate
publication that helps small service businesses pick software. It currently covers
three tools: Fillout (intake forms), Softr (client portals), Noloco (portals + team ops),
and is named to grow past them.
Since 27 Oct 2026 a blog (`/blog/`) carries shorter, dated posts on the wider job:
intake, portals, operations, AI builders and AI policy, web basics and buying
software. Posts follow the same sourcing rules and point to the guides and
comparisons for decisions.
Success is a reader reaching a confident decision and clicking through to the
vendor — earned by honest, sourced, dated information, not persuasion. The site
never invents screenshots, testimonials, ratings, results, or affiliate URLs.

## Brand Personality

The mechanic's estimate. Concrete, verified, unafraid to say "don't buy."
Evidence comes before opinion: claims are dated, sourced, and stated plainly.
Recommendations are decisive when the evidence supports them,while uncertainty
and incomplete evidence are acknowledged explicitly. No hype, manufactured wit,
vague hedging, or filler.
This personality describes the editorial attitude, not a literal visual metaphor.
Do not turn the site into an invoice, workshop, industrial, mechanic, newspaper-ledger,
or utilitarian aesthetic. The visual identity should remain premium, modern,
product-forward, and editorial.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing templates (hero-metrics, identical card grids, eyebrow
  kickers on every section).
- Text-wall docs portals and the site's own rejected v1–v4 iterations: layouts
  where products are implied but never seen.
- Invented social proof, fake screenshots, vague "best tool" listicles.
- The editorial-typographic monoculture (display serif + italic + mono labels +
  ruled columns) as a reflex rather than a choice.
- The warm cream/sand near-white AI-default palette.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the product, don't describe it.** Real UI imagery — vendor-published
   until owner captures land — is present on every key page. No placeholder
   frames shipping as content.
2. **Numbers before adjectives.** Verified prices, limits, and check dates lead;
   every claim traces to a source. The pricing table is a hero artifact, not a
   footnote.
3. **Decide with the reader.** Every page ends in a decision — "pick X if…",
   "when another tool wins", including "buy nothing". A reader who leaves
   without purchasing is still a success if they left knowing why.
4. **Earn the click.** Independence is stated plainly; affiliate status is
   disclosed; official links until real affiliate URLs exist.
5. **Dense but scannable.** Compressed copy, tables, and real artifacts over
   paragraphs. Every screen rewards a skim and a read. Density must not become
   visual compression: important sections need deliberate breathing room, hierarchy,
   and variation in rhythm.
6. **Produced, not raw.** Product media is staged and composed (color fields,
   angle, overlap, bleed, recorded loops) the way Airtable and Customer.io stage
   theirs. Gradient fields, grain, texture and glow are allowed where they serve
   that staging (owner decision, 30 Sep 2026; the earlier flat-only rule is
   withdrawn). The material stays real: vendor-published assets or our own
   captures, logged in `src/data/media-manifest.ts`.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA target. Dark theme is a first-class mode (owner requirement), not an
afterthought. All animation has a reduced-motion alternative. Body text ≥4.5:1
contrast. No content gated behind JavaScript — the site is static HTML with JS
only for real interactions.
