AI app builders get the headlines. The quieter change is AI inside the tools you already run. Noloco's workflow guide lists a set of AI actions next to the usual create, update and email steps: **Ask ChatGPT**, **Summarize Text**, **Correct Grammar**, **Extract Keywords** and **Finish Text**.

These are small. That is their strength. A workflow can take a field, run one of these actions on it, and write the result into another field, the same way it would copy a value or send an email.

## Five jobs worth automating

**1. Summarise a long brief.** When an enquiry arrives with three paragraphs of context, a two-line summary in its own field lets the person triaging the inbox decide faster. They still open the full brief before replying.

**2. Tag enquiries for sorting.** Extracting keywords from a brief ("e-commerce", "rebrand", "Shopify") gives you a column to filter by. It will not be perfect. It does not need to be: it is a sorting aid, not a decision.

**3. Clean up internal notes.** Notes typed between calls are often fragments. A grammar pass makes them readable for the colleague who takes over the account.

**4. Draft a first reply for a person to edit.** A "finish text" or prompt action can turn bullet points into a polite paragraph. Store it in a draft field. A person edits and sends it.

**5. Turn meeting notes into next steps.** Ask for a short list of actions from a notes field, written into a separate field your team reviews before creating tasks.

The pattern in all five: AI writes into a field your team reads. Nothing goes straight to a client.

## Two jobs to keep human

**Anything a client reads without review.** A summary that misreads a brief is harmless in an internal field. The same summary in an email to the client is a mistake with your name on it.

**Anything that changes access or money.** Do not let AI output decide a user's role, a project's company or an invoice amount. Those fields drive permissions and billing. A wrong value there is a data leak or a dispute.

## Watch the runs

AI actions live inside workflows, and workflows have limits. Noloco Free includes 100 workflow runs a month and stops when they run out; Build includes 3,000 and bills extra runs. An "on every update" trigger with an AI step can use runs quickly. Trigger AI actions on a specific change, such as a new enquiry or a status moving to "Brief received", rather than on any edit. Our post on [estimating workflow runs](/blog/workflow-runs-explained/) has a worksheet.

## Check the data rule first

Text you send to an AI action leaves your table and passes through a model provider. Before you automate it, decide which fields may go through. A short [AI data policy](/blog/ai-client-data-policy/) answers that once for the whole team, so nobody has to decide case by case inside the workflow editor.

## A small test before you rely on it

Run the workflow on ten real, older records and read every output next to the source. Count how many you would have sent, edited or thrown away. If more than a couple need throwing away, narrow the action's job or keep it manual. If most are fine, keep the human review step anyway. It costs a few seconds and catches the one that matters.
