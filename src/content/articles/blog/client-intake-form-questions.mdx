An intake form has two readers. The client fills it in, and someone on your team turns it into a quote. Most forms are written for neither. They ask what a brand questionnaire asks, at a length nobody finishes, and the answers still need a call to make sense.

A better test for every question is simple: **what does this answer decide?** If you cannot name the decision, the question can go.

## The core questions

These are the questions most service businesses need before they can reply with a price or a "not for us". Adjust the wording to your trade.

| Question | Field type | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name and email | Text, email | Who to reply to |
| Company or project name | Text | How the record is filed |
| What do you need? | Single choice of your services | Which branch of the form, and who on the team reads it |
| What does success look like in three months? | Short text | Whether you can deliver it |
| Budget range | Single choice of your real ranges | Whether to quote, and which package |
| Ideal start date | Date | Whether you have capacity |
| Hard deadline, if any | Date, optional | Rush pricing or a polite no |
| Existing material | File upload, optional | How much discovery the job needs |

Eight questions. A client can finish them in a few minutes, and the record they create is enough for a first reply.

## Two conditional branches that pay for themselves

Use logic so each client only sees the questions for their job. Fillout documents two kinds: conditional fields that show or hide based on earlier answers, and page logic that sends the respondent to a different page.

**Branch on the service.** A website project needs page count, platform and integrations. A brand project needs deliverables and usage. Put each set on its own page and route by the "What do you need?" answer.

**Branch on the budget.** If the range is below your minimum, show a short, honest note: what you can do at that level, or who you would refer them to. It saves both sides a call.

## Questions to move to the call

These are good questions. They are just better asked by a person:

- **"Tell us about your brand."** The answer is long, vague and different every time. Ask it live.
- **"Who are your competitors?"** Useful for the work, not for the quote.
- **"How did you hear about us?"** Capture it from the link instead with a [hidden field](/blog/hidden-fields-lead-source/).
- **"What is your timeline?"** Replace with two date fields. "Soon" is not a timeline.
- **"Anything else?"** Keep one optional box at the end. Do not make it required.

## Questions that need care

**Uploads.** Files are useful, but check the limits. Fillout lists 20 MB per file on Free, Starter and Pro, and 1 GB on Business and Team. A video brief will not fit on the lower plans. Ask for a link instead if you expect large files.

**Personal or sensitive data.** If you do not need it to quote, do not collect it in the form. Less data in the record is less data to protect.

**Legal or contract terms.** A form is not a contract. Link to your terms and keep the form about the job.

## Check the brief, not the form

When the form is ready, submit three fictional briefs: a perfect fit, a poor fit and a confusing one. Then read the records as your colleague would, without the form open.

- Can you quote from the record alone?
- Does the poor fit get a clear, kind answer from the form itself?
- Did the confusing brief reveal a question that needs a choice instead of free text?
