Most service businesses take money after a proposal. Some take it before the first meeting. Photographers book dates with a retainer. Consultants sell a paid audit. Studios charge for a discovery workshop that becomes the first phase of the project.

If that describes your first step, the intake form is a natural place to collect the payment. The client fills in the brief and pays in the same session, and you never send a separate invoice for a small amount.

## When a deposit belongs in the form

A payment step is a good fit when all three of these are true:

1. **The first step has a fixed price.** You can name it before you talk to the client.
2. **You commit something scarce when they book.** A date, a slot in your calendar, a workshop seat.
3. **You would turn away people who will not pay.** The deposit is a filter, not an obstacle.

It is a poor fit when the scope depends on the brief. If you need a call to quote, asking for money first will lose good clients who want to talk before they commit.

## What the form tools support

Both form builders we cover document a Stripe payment step:

| | Fillout | Tally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment step | A payment page with Stripe, on every plan | Stripe payment forms, on the free plan |
| Extra fee from the form tool | None documented | None on top of Stripe's fee |
| Route payers and non-payers differently | Page logic can branch before the payment page | Conditional logic |

Stripe charges its own processing fees in both cases. Check Stripe's current rates for your country.

The portal tools are different. Neither Softr nor Noloco documents a payment step inside a form, which is why a deposit is one of the clearest reasons to add a separate form tool to a portal setup.

## Design the payment step so people finish it

**Put the price before the form, not at the end.** A client who learns the cost on the last page feels tricked. State it on the first screen, then again on the payment page.

**Ask for the payment last.** Collect the brief first, then the deposit. If someone drops at the payment step, you still have their answers and can follow up.

**Branch around it when it does not apply.** With page logic, a returning client or a referral partner can skip the payment page. Only the people who should pay see it.

**Say what happens next.** The confirmation should tell the client when they will hear from you and what the deposit covers.

## Write the refund rule before you take the money

A deposit raises a question the form cannot answer: what happens if either side cancels? Decide before you launch:

- Is the deposit refundable, and until when?
- Does it count toward the project price?
- What happens if you are the one who cannot proceed?

Put the answer in one or two sentences next to the payment step and in the confirmation email. This is a business decision, not a software setting, and local consumer rules may apply. Check them for your country.

## A setup in four steps

1. Connect Stripe to the form tool and run a test payment in Stripe's test mode.
2. Add the payment page after the brief questions.
3. Map the payment result to a field in your table so staff can see who paid.
4. Send a test enquiry through every branch: one that pays, one that skips, one that abandons at the payment page.
