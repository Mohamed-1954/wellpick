Two of the tools around the products we cover changed hands or names in 2026.

On 4 September 2026, Bending Spoons said it had completed its acquisition of Airtable, which it describes as "the platform used by more than 500,000 organizations". The announcement quotes Bending Spoons' CEO saying the focus will be on "investing heavily in Airtable's product, customer support, and go-to-market capabilities."

In March 2026, Fillout's parent company renamed itself Zite. Its announcement says Fillout "remains its own product under the Zite umbrella." Zite also sells its own AI app and portal builder, which competes with the portal tools many Fillout users connect to.

Neither event broke anything. Both are a good moment to check how dependent your client work is on one company's next decision.

## What usually stays the same, at first

Your account, your data and your current plan keep working. Announcements in both cases speak of continuity: investment in Airtable's product, and Fillout continuing as its own product. There is no reason to migrate anything because of a headline.

## What can change later

We are not predicting any change. These are the parts of a software relationship that any owner can adjust over time, so they are the parts worth watching:

- **Plans and prices.** Tiers get renamed, features move between plans, and seat rules change.
- **Product focus.** A company that sells several products decides where its engineers go.
- **Integrations.** A connection between two tools depends on both companies keeping it.
- **Terms.** Data processing, support levels and affiliate programs all live in documents that can be updated.

## A checklist for any client-facing tool

**1. Record what you pay for today.** Plan name, price, billing period, user counts and the limits you depend on. Take a screenshot of the pricing page with the date. If the plan changes, you will know exactly what moved.

**2. Note your renewal date.** Yearly plans renew in one payment. Re-read the pricing page a month before.

**3. Test your export.** Export a small set of real records and open the file. Noloco documents CSV exports that keep linked-record IDs. Softr's community team said in 2024 that a Softr app cannot be exported as code, so with Softr your exit runs through your data source. Know which applies to you. Our post on [exit plans for no-code apps](/blog/exit-plan-no-code-apps/) goes further.

**4. List the integrations that carry client data.** Form to table, table to portal, portal to email. Each link is a dependency on two vendors.

**5. Keep the source of truth where you control it.** If your records live in a base you can export, a change in the portal layer is an inconvenience, not a rebuild.

## What this means for the tools we cover

For Fillout users connecting to Softr or Noloco, the practical question is whether Fillout keeps investing in the integrations you use. Today, Fillout documents writing to Airtable and embedding in Softr. Check those help pages again when you renew.

For Airtable users running a portal on top, the question is the same one you should ask anyway: if Airtable's plans changed, which of your setups would need a different tier? [Airtable Portals vs Softr](/compare/airtable-portals-vs-softr/) lists the Airtable billing rules that matter for client access today.

Ownership news is a reminder, not an emergency. The businesses that handle it calmly are the ones that already know what they pay for and how they would leave.
