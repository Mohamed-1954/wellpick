Portal pricing pages look simple: a plan, a price, a number of users. The trap is the word "user". Each vendor defines it differently, and the definition decides which plan you need.

Here is how the three portal routes we cover count people, as their pricing pages and docs describe it on 27 October 2026.

## Softr: team or client, by email domain

Softr splits app users into **team users** and **client users**. A team user shares a business email domain with a builder of the app. Everyone else is a client user.

That has a side effect small teams miss: a colleague who signs in with a Gmail address counts as a client user, not a team user.

| Plan | Team users | Client users | Extra users |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | 5 app users in total | | None |
| Basic | 5 | 5 | Cannot buy more |
| Pro | 10 | 50 | $3 per team user, $1 per client user, per month |
| Business | 30 | 100 | $5 per team user, $2 per client user, per month |

Two more rules matter. A user is counted once per unique email across all your Softr apps, and deactivated users do not count. And past a plan's maximum, new users are created but stay deactivated: the invitation goes out, and the client still cannot sign in.

## Noloco: a seat starts at the first login

Noloco counts a seat once a user logs in. An invited client who never signs in does not use one. Team members are users on the workspace's email domain, or users with a team member or data admin role. Everyone else is a client.

- **Free:** unlimited team seats and 50 active clients.
- **Build:** 10 team seats, then $5 each billed yearly or $6 monthly, and up to 1,000 active clients.

Deactivating a user frees the seat from the next billing cycle, and their records stay. That makes Noloco forgiving for seasonal clients, as long as you remember to deactivate.

## Airtable Portals: readers are free, editors are not

Airtable's Portals add-on bills differently again. Its help article says read-only portal users are not billable. Portal users who can edit or comment use a portal seat, and seats come in packs. The 15-seat pack is listed at $120 a month on the Team plan.

The catch is in the word "comment". A client who can only leave comments still uses a seat. So does a client who submits a form inside an interface, because that needs editor access.

## Fillout: no users to count

Fillout, the form tool, does not charge per seat. Its plans are set by monthly responses and features. If your clients only send you things and never sign in to look, a form may be all you need.

## Count before you compare

Write down three numbers before you open any pricing page:

1. **Staff who will sign in,** and which email domain each one uses.
2. **Client contacts who will sign in,** not client companies. One company can mean four logins.
3. **What clients will do:** read only, comment, edit or submit.

Then run each vendor's rule over the same list. A studio with 12 client contacts who only read looks very different on Airtable Portals (inside the smallest pack) and on Softr (past Basic's 5 client users, so Pro).
