## Decide where the form belongs first

A public enquiry and a logged-in project update are different jobs. The first accepts information from a stranger; the second can expose and change an existing client record. Design the access boundary before comparing themes or field animations.

There are actually three options: **standalone Softr Forms** (outside an app), the **Conditional Form Block** (inside an app), and **Fillout** (a separate service with a documented Softr embed path).

## Match the route to the scenario

- **A straightforward new-client brief:** use standalone Softr Forms if you already build in Softr. Fillout earns its place here only if your team already maintains its forms there or needs an integration Softr lacks.
- **Several contacts or related items per submission:** this is where Fillout is documented and Softr’s forms are not (linked-record subforms, combined and repeated forms). Before paying, write down what should happen for one item, several items and a repeated submission.
- **An existing client changes a brief:** start with the record-access model. Fillout warns that an Airtable update link can expose every field on the record, including fields not shown as questions; its mitigations are separate private tables or restricted prefilled data.
- **A large upload or a specific login method:** Fillout lists 20 MB per file on Free, Starter and Pro, 1 GB on Business and Team, and 5 GB on Enterprise. Login pages are available on Starter, Pro, Business and Enterprise. These requirements can choose the plan before submission volume does. Starter costs $15/month billed yearly or $19/month billed monthly; Business costs $75 or $89.

## Make the choice

Write down the same five requirements for both options: the questions, the records created or updated, the identity requirement, the largest upload, and the next action. Build only that path in each option you are seriously considering.

Choose the native option if it meets those needs and the maintainer understands it. Choose Fillout if it solves an unmet need at a cost you can justify. Keep your current form if neither creates a clear benefit.

One case needs its own check. If you plan to rely on Softr’s in-app Conditional Form Block, confirm its plan with Softr before you buy. The pricing page and the docs give different answers. If the block turns out to need Pro, the gap between Softr Basic and Pro is $80 a month on yearly billing ($19 vs $99). Fillout’s conditional logic works on every plan, Free included, and its Softr embed needs a paid Softr account, which Basic is. So Softr Basic plus Fillout costs $19 a month with Fillout Free, or $34 with Starter’s login pages, against $99 for Softr Pro, as long as Basic’s 5 team and 5 client users are enough.
