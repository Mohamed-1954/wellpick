## Do you need a portal at all?

A portal earns its place when clients return to the same project information and need their own view of a shared system. An intake form solves an earlier problem, collecting the brief, and does not by itself control who can see the resulting records.

- **Keep your current setup** if a shared folder and a clear weekly update already work. New software adds another thing to maintain.
- **Fix intake first** if the main problem is incomplete briefs. Start with the [intake form guide](/guides/fillout-intake-to-airtable/), or check whether free [Tally is enough](/compare/fillout-vs-tally/).
- **Evaluate a portal** if clients repeatedly need project status, deliverables, or a next action, and those records need per-client access rules.

A portal will not fix stale information. Assign someone to own updates before you assign software to display them.

Two routes sit outside this site's three tools. If your records already live in Airtable, its own Portals add-on may be enough: see [Airtable Portals vs Softr and Noloco](/compare/airtable-portals-vs-softr/). If you want proposals, contracts, invoicing and a portal in one subscription, all-in-one client tools such as SuiteDash are a different category, which this site does not cover.

## Pick your path

### Path 1: intake only, [Fillout](/fillout/)

You are here if…

- Briefs arrive with missing pieces and your team re-asks the same questions.
- The brief should write straight into Airtable or another system of record.
- Nobody is asking for a client-facing project view yet.

Build guide: [Fillout intake form into Airtable](/guides/fillout-intake-to-airtable/).

### Path 2: portal on existing records, [Softr](/softr/)

You are here if…

- Clients keep asking for status, deliverables, or what happens next.
- Your project records already live in Airtable, Google Sheets, or a database you control.
- One person can own the portal build and its upkeep.

Build guide: [Build a Softr client portal](/guides/build-softr-client-portal/).

### Path 3: portal plus team operations, [Noloco](/noloco/)

You are here if…

- Staff run CRM, projects, and tasks in one place and clients should see a slice of the same records.
- You need record- and field-level permissions between the two audiences.
- Your team is willing to maintain the data model behind it.

Build guide: [Build a Noloco client portal](/guides/build-noloco-client-portal/). Not sure where the line between Softr and Noloco falls? Read [Softr vs Noloco](/compare/softr-vs-noloco/): the 6th client login is usually the tipping point.

## Write a one-page requirement

Use this deliberately small example as a starting point. It is a proposed workflow, not a customer case study:

> A small studio wants each client to see their own projects, open the latest deliverable, and send feedback. Staff can update progress. Clients cannot see another company’s projects or internal notes.

Then make the boundaries explicit: who maintains the portal, where the records live, which actions each side can take, how access is scoped to a company, and what happens when a project ends or a contact leaves.

Do not use a client’s editable form answer as the authority for granting access to a company. Have an internal owner verify that relationship.

## Set your stop conditions before a trial

A short trial should answer a decision, not produce an attractive dashboard. Use two synthetic client companies and disqualify the setup if:

- a client can retrieve another client’s record or file;
- a required action cannot be restricted on an affordable plan;
- staff must copy information between systems often enough to make it unreliable;
- you cannot recover the records and relationships needed to leave.
